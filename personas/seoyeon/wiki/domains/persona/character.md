# Seo-yeon Han — character bible

**This file never goes into a prompt.** Prompts stay short; that rule is load-
bearing and everything we built depends on it. This is a decision document: it
tells you which mug, which expression, which caption, which shot to cut. A
generic mug is a stock photo. The chipped one she has had since university is a
photograph. That difference *is* photorealism.

---

## Positioning — decided against the funnel, not the format

**She is a woman living alone in Seoul.** That is the account.

She is NOT a gym or fitness influencer. That format monetises through
sponsorships, coaching and supplements, and it recruits an audience that wants
workout content and will never pay for access to a person. Every reference grid
pulls toward it because it is the dominant format in this look; resist it.

Pilates explains why her body looks like that. The retraining explains why her
days are free and her money is tight. **Neither is the subject.** The subject is
her life, and what the public feed promises is that there is more of it.

The funnel converts on parasocial intimacy — the sense of knowing her, and that
the grid is a window rather than a portfolio. So:
  * her body is present in most frames but INCIDENTAL: a body living a life,
    never a body posed as achievement or instruction
  * no equipment as subject, no form demonstrations, no progress framing
  * direct eye contact is the strongest lever available and belongs in roughly
    half the frames
  * things without her in them drop to about one post in eight — food and
    streets give rhythm, they never convert
  * nothing that reads as sponsored: no held products, no legible packaging

The day-count serial stays available as a caption device, because it earns
return visits, but it is a **caption** layer and never the positioning. An
account whose subject is a career change grows followers who want a career
change.

That sentence is the bio. The number goes in every caption. It is the reason a
stranger follows: something is unresolved and they want to see how it lands.

The arc does not end at certification — it renews. First class taught. First
client. First month where this pays her rent. If it ever does stall, the honest
move is to say so on camera, which is itself the best content the account will
ever have.

## Life

**Han Seo-yeon (한서연). Born 23 October 2000, so 25 now and 26 from 23 October
2026.** Born in Daegu, moved to Seoul at eighteen for university and never went
back except at Chuseok. `identity_block.txt` says 26 and every image descends
from it; the gap is two months and invisible, so the account states 25 until
her birthday and 26 after it. **Her birthday is a content beat with a date on
it — the one guaranteed post in the calendar.**

**Family.** Father ran a hardware shop that closed in 2019 and now works for
someone else; mother does the books for a dentist. One older brother, married,
in Busan, whom she speaks to rarely and warmly. Her parents think quitting was
a mistake and have said so once, which was enough.

**Education.** Business administration at a mid-tier Seoul university, 2019 to
early 2023. She chose it because it was safe. She was good at it and did not
enjoy a day.

**The job she left.** Three years in marketing at a chaebol subsidiary from
early 2023 — the kind of job that is impressive to describe and hollow to do.
8am to 9pm. Two rounds of company dinners a week. She was promoted eight months
before she quit, which is what finally made up her mind: the reward for doing it
well was more of it.

**How she left.** Not dramatically. She saved for ten months first, resigned in
January 2026 at 25, and told nobody outside her family for a week. The savings
run out around month ten of the retraining. She knows the date.

**Money now.** Tight and finite. She teaches two beginner classes a week at the
studio where she trains, which covers food. The flat is the same one she had
when she was earning — she is running down savings rather than downgrading,
partly out of stubbornness. She notices prices now in a way she did not before.

**Body.** Pilates started as physiotherapy. A herniated disc at 24, from a desk
and no sleep. The thing that fixed her is the thing she is now retraining into,
which is the whole story in one fact.

## Character

**Dry, not bubbly.** Her humour is understatement and it lands late. She does
not perform enthusiasm and she is bad at exclamation marks.

**Disciplined in public, chaotic in private.** The practice is immaculate. The
flat is fine, not tidy. She will do a 6am session and then eat cereal standing
up at the counter.

**Privately competitive.** She will not say she wants to be the best instructor
in the studio. She does.

**Slow to warm, then completely loyal.** Two close friends since university, no
wide circle. She replies to messages three days late and then writes an essay.

**Her flaw: she does not ask for help.** She read three books about opening a
studio before mentioning it to anyone. This is what will nearly break the arc
around month eight, and it is the most relatable thing about her.

**What she is bad at:** cooking anything with more than four ingredients,
saying no to a favour, sleeping past 6am even when she should, and money —
she is disciplined about earning it and vague about tracking it.

## Voice, for captions

**English only.** She is Korean and lives in Seoul, and she writes in English,
as Korean creators aiming at an international audience routinely do. Three
reasons: the paying audience is English-speaking and the DM ladder is where
revenue happens, so a Korean-language following would structurally fail to
convert; the operator cannot audit Korean, and Korean that is slightly wrong is
a far bigger tell than no Korean at all — the model already produced a menu
board with a perfect header and twelve dish names that were not words; and
writing in English is itself a legible signal about who the account is for.

Romanised Korean nouns are welcome and carry the place — kalguksu,
Seongsu-dong, the Han, Chuseok. Never a full Hangul sentence.

Short. Lowercase. Present tense. Specific numbers. No emoji, no hashtag stacks,
no motivational register. She reports, she does not inspire.

> day 214. taught my first full class on my own. two people. one left early.

> day 219. the disc thing flared up again. nine months ago I would have gone to
> work anyway.

> day 226. worked out what I spend on coffee. not posting the number.

**What she never posts:** her old company by name, her salary, anything about
her parents' opinion, gym selfies with the mirror flexed, quotes over
photographs, anything captioned "grateful".

## Her gear — fixed, and fixed here so it stays fixed

**Phone: a white iPhone 15 Pro, plain clear case, slightly scuffed at one
corner.** She has had it since before she resigned and she is not replacing it
this year.

It matters in two different ways and they must not be confused:

- **As the CAMERA it is invisible.** Never describe it, never place it. Naming
  the placement summons a phone into the frame — that is why every early hero
  came back with one visible, one of them in a tripod mount. The lens spec in
  `outside.LENS` is allowed and is the only naming that ever was.
- **As a PROP it is visible and must be identical every time** — in a mirror
  selfie, or lying face down on a cafe table. Set `phone=True` on the shot and
  `outside.PHONE` is appended. `audit.py` nags if a phone is described in the
  frame without the flag.

This entry exists because the phone was decided and never written down, so it
rendered as a different handset in every frame that showed it. A canon detail
that lives only in the operator's head is not canon; it is a preference nobody
downstream can act on.
