## Season shift
The heat broke last Friday. Monday morning the window was open when she woke
up for the first time since July. It is not cold yet — the afternoons are
still warm — but the air is different, the light is lower and gold in the
evening, and the standing fan is running a gear slower. A season changing is
the first real thing that happens this week.

## Thread from W37

## What happens to her, Monday to Sunday

**MON 14 — day 251.** First morning of the fourth slot. She is at the studio
before anyone else, at ten to seven, standing in the morning light from the
tall windows. The room is empty and for a minute she just stands there, which
is when she takes it.

**TUE 15 — day 252.** Freelance marketing at the table. Laptop, one pear from
the Saturday market, a glass of water. The standing fan is running but the
window is cracked open behind the curtain — two degrees of cooler air coming
through the gap and she can feel it on her shoulder. She takes the table from
above, phone held straight down, no face.

**WED 16 — day 253.** Observation hours all day — eight of them, in the studio
watching the senior instructor run three sessions back to back. She fills a
page of notes she will not be able to read tomorrow. By 18:30 she is on the
stairwell landing sitting against the wall, face down, the notebook open on
her knee, her phone in her hand. She photographs the landing from her own
eye-line because it costs nothing to document.

**THU 17 — day 254.** Finished work at six and walked down to the river. The
Han is different in September: the light goes flat and gold, the air is dry,
and there are fewer people on the path. She bought a pear from the ajumma
with the cart by the bridge and ate it standing. Profile shot, east toward the
bridge with the lamps not quite on yet.

**FRI 18 — day 255.** The exam is late October and she has not touched the
binder since August. Friday night at the desk — open binder, highlighter
caps on the wood, her hand mid-sentence, coffee gone cold. She takes the frame
from above, her hand filling the near half, the binder open to a page of
muscle-grouping diagrams, the coffee mug out of focus beyond.

**SAT 19 — day 256.** Course module all day, eight to six. By the time she is
out her voice is gone from answering questions all afternoon and her back
hurts where the disc thing lives. In the empty hallway after everyone has left,
she leans against the wall and takes one looking straight into the lens —
the face of someone who knows she has another one of these next weekend and is
not thinking about it yet.

**SUN 20 — day 257.** Sunday reset. She wakes up late for her (9am, which is
late), pulls on an oversized tee, ties her hair up in a high messy bun, and
does the floor — laundry, grocery bag unpacked from yesterday, the tote for
Monday. 3/4 face, caught in the middle of the reset, the room around her mid-
tidy, the new box of pears on the counter visible beyond her shoulder.
